# myCobot 280 Policy Claim Boundary

**Prepared:** 2026-08-01 PDT

## Evaluation Contract

The no-fine-tuning, deterministic-fine-tuning, and randomized-fine-tuning
control uses the same 11 fresh randomized candidates, close 256 x 256 camera,
530-step horizon, prompt, and exact-7D state/action contract. Candidate seeds
`92000-92010` are direct, unfiltered draws with no overlap with source-dataset
attempt seeds. Every policy rollout is counted, including failures.

Strict success requires the pickup, lift, two-pad contact, and hold gates plus a
maximum adaptive-pad-to-cube penetration of at most 3.0 mm. Functional
pickup-and-hold applies the same task gates but excludes penetration.

## Supported Claims

| Question | Evidence | Supported statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine-tuning vs no fine-tuning | Matched seed `20260731`: base 0/11 pickup+hold and 0/11 strict; both fine-tuned policies 11/11 pickup+hold | Fine-tuning changes task behavior and enables pickup-and-hold on this matched schedule. |
| Randomized vs deterministic, strict | Fresh randomized physics: randomized strict success exceeds deterministic in 3/3 paired training seeds; pooled 14/33 vs 4/33 | Randomized training has a directionally replicated strict-success advantage in this experiment. |
| Randomized vs deterministic, penetration excluded | Fresh randomized physics: 33/33 vs 32/33 pickup+hold | No material functional-task advantage is established after excluding penetration. |
| Contact quality | Mean paired maximum penetration changes by -0.082 mm and penetration-only failures fall from 28/33 to 19/33 | The measured randomized-training advantage is principally cleaner contact under the fixed 3 mm gate. |

The three independent paired signs give exact two-sided `p=0.25`. Therefore
the randomized-over-deterministic result is replicated engineering evidence,
not statistical significance or a population-level effect estimate.

## Teacher-Success Conditioning

Teacher-success filtering applies to the randomized demonstrations used for
training and validation, not to policy evaluation. The source generator
attempted 73 candidates and retained 60 teacher successes: 50 train and 10
validation. All 13 rejected attempts are in the 0.034-0.036 kg mass quartile,
where only 2/15 attempts were accepted. Validation contains no accepted
high-mass examples and no examples in the first two friction quartiles.

Consequently, this experiment compares learning from successful deterministic
versus successful randomized demonstrations. It does not measure learning from
the declared randomized physics distribution uniformly, and it must not report
the teacher's 60/73 acceptance as policy success.

## Assumptions And Non-Claims

- The 3.0 mm penetration cap remains the preregistered strict threshold.
- The penetration metric covers adaptive-pad-to-cube contact only; it does not
  measure cube-table/mat overlap or robot self-collision.
- Camera, policy architecture, action/state dimensionality, training steps,
  and evaluation schedules are matched between fine-tuned policies.
- Held-out supervised loss and action RMSE do not consistently rank the two
  fine-tuned policies and are not treated as task-success proxies.
- The experiment does not establish uniform high-mass or low-friction
  robustness, real-robot transfer, optimized convergence, or agentic benefit.
